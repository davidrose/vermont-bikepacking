The Night Before
Arrive in Burlington · Set up camp · Explore the waterfront
✦
North Beach Campground is Burlington's best-kept secret — 69 tent sites on a sandy Lake Champlain beach, minutes from downtown via a paved bike path. The sunsets over the Adirondacks are the warm-up act. Set up camp, walk to the waterfront for dinner, and wake up ready to ride. It's also just a short ride south to Airport Park where Day 1 begins.
PMarrive
North Beach CampgroundNight 0
Burlington. 69 tent sites on a sandy Lake Champlain beach. Urban campground with rural character — bike path connects directly to downtown Burlington and the Island Line Trail. Adirondack sunset views. Hot showers, restrooms. 10 min bike ride to Airport Park. ☎ (802) 862-0942
Eve
Burlington WaterfrontDinner
Bike or walk to Church Street for dinner. Hen of the Wood for farm-to-table, Penny Cluse Café for comfort food, or grab pizza at American Flatbread on the waterfront. Stock up on snacks at City Market co-op.

The Lake Crossing
Airport Park → Brewster River Campground · ~42 mi · 8 mi Island Line · 14 mi road · 20 mi LVRT
✦
The causeway is three miles of marble rubble laid in 1899, hovering inches above Lake Champlain. The bike ferry at "The Cut" costs ~$8 RT and feels like a portal. Your first night is at a campground the owners discovered on a bikepacking trip during the pandemic — riverside tent sites, a 20-foot waterfall, fireflies in the meadow, and a Bernese Mountain Dog named Waffles who may greet you at the gate.

Into the Kingdom
Brewster River → New Discovery State Park · ~55 mi · 45 mi LVRT · 10 mi road
✦
This is the big day — 55 miles, but the LVRT does the work. The grade never exceeds 3%. You'll ride through 12-foot corn stalks, past the Fisher Covered Bridge (the last working covered railroad bridge in the country), and into the Northeast Kingdom. At Danville, peel south on VT-232 into Groton State Forest — 26,000 acres of CCC-era Vermont, where the lean-tos have stone fireplaces built by hand in the 1930s.

The Easy Return
New Discovery → Elmore State Park · ~30 mi · 10 mi road · 15 mi LVRT · 5 mi road
✦
The payoff day. Retrace north to the LVRT, then let gravity do the work heading west. The 500 feet you climbed over two days now unspools behind you. Between Hyde Park and Johnson, pull off at Dog's Head Falls for a river swim. End at Elmore — "The Beauty Spot of Vermont" — where the CCC built a beach house in 1936 that still has a concession stand and boat rentals.

Back Across the Lake
Elmore → Airport Park · ~38 mi · 5 mi road · 20 mi LVRT/road · 8 mi Island Line
✦
The last day is the shortest and the sweetest. You already know the trail, the road, the causeway. But it hits different heading home — the lake is bigger when you're tired, and the moment you pull into Airport Park you'll feel the particular satisfaction of a loop closed. Three state parks, one riverside campground, zero RV hookups, and more Vermont than most people see in a lifetime.
